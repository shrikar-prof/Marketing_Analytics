PART AThe answer in plain language

A

Do not reallocate yet; the model forecasts well but cannot rank channels

The question as the client asked it

A consumer brand spends about $34K a week across TV, out-of-home (billboards; OOH), print, Facebook and search. Finance wants proof the split is right before approving next year's plan. What does each channel add to revenue, where does each saturate, and how should the same total be split? (problem_description.md)

The answer

Do not move money between channels yet. The model forecasts weekly revenue well, but it cannot tell which way a budget shift would move revenue. The business already ran a natural test: weekly media spend fell 72% and like-for-like revenue fell 3.1%. Most of the cut was billboards. Hold the 2019 mix, keep billboards behind a test gate, and use the gap up to $34K a week for regional tests. Those tests, not this model, should decide the split.

What should happen next

  1. Correct the record to Finance: withdraw the 18% event lift, the "flat budget" claim and the "TV to Facebook is positive" claim. MEMO §4 B4
  2. Freeze the 2019 mix ($28.2K a week) as next year's base; no billboard spend above 2019 without a passed test. MEMO §4 B1, B3
  3. Ring-fence the $5.8K a week gap to the $34K plan (about $0.30M a year, assumed) as test money only. MEMO §4 B2
  4. Run four tests in 90 days: TV off in some regions, print off in others, a Facebook lift test, a brand-search pause. MEMO §5
  5. Rebuild the model as a regional Bayesian model and feed it the test results. MEMO §4 A2–A3

What is uncertain

  • Both datasets are simulated; no number describes a real brand.
  • The model has no carry-over term, so TV and print may be understated by up to about 2x assumed.
  • The 50% gross margin behind every break-even rule is assumed.

B3

A simple Ridge model won narrowly and forecasts held-out weeks within 6%

The model predicts weekly revenue from spend, season and market conditions. It learned from the first 188 weeks and was tested once on the last 20 (May–Nov 2019). It uses 15 inputs built on the log of spend. It does not use the carry-over or Hill saturation terms that were built. MC; FU R1·1

Five families were compared on rolling-origin cross-validation. The model card says 10 splits; the follow-up counted 11. MC; FU R1 correction 6

Decision rules assumed 50% margin

  • Scale a channel when a test's 90% lower bound on return is at least $2.00 per $1.
  • Cut search or Facebook when the upper bound is below $2.00.
  • Cut TV or print only when the upper bound is below $1.00, to allow for long-term effects.
  • Otherwise hold and extend the test. MEMO §3.3
